Why we build the commons first

The strands of
our philosophy.

A successful public space is not measured by acreage alone — but by how deeply it roots into human life and local culture.

01

Open design
reduces isolation.

Standard urban expansion patterns often force homes to abut one another blindly, leaving residents disconnected from shared spaces and natural layers.

Building open-air plazas and neighbourhood commons first establishes a canvas for organic, daily human interaction — directly reducing social isolation and building mutual trust.

02

Art and nature
restore the mind.

Integrating open amphitheaters and craft spaces directly into restored forest buffers lets local culture evolve naturally.

Stepping into a quiet sanctuary filled with natural sounds — birds chirping, rain, rustling leaves — acts as a powerful, restorative shield against the mental fatigue of cramped city environments.

03

Shared space builds
collective will.

When families regularly step out of private enclaves to interact within shared cultural spaces, they move away from individual escapism.

Surrounded by art and nature, people naturally begin to share diverse viewpoints, face challenges together, and co-create sustainable solutions as a collective.

Proposed Nodes

in the growth corridor

Each site chosen for ecological value — wetlands, stream confluences, historic landscapes.

01
Eco-Park Dedication

The Salumaradha Thimmakka Biodiversity Corridor

Hejjala Village · Bengaluru South

Hejjala sits at the confluence of two prominent seasonal streams feeding the Arkavathi catchment. Three existing lakes — under pressure from encroachment and siltation — are at a critical juncture. We propose formally dedicating this corridor to Salumaradha Thimmakka, honouring her lifelong legacy.

Hejjala Land Study — Coming Soon
02
Public Environmental Designation

The Chinnakuruchi Wetland Sanctuary

Chinnakuruchi Village · Bengaluru South

This node centres on conserving the vulnerable wetlands that naturally weave around the agricultural patches of Chinnakuruchi's villages. Preserving these water bodies before outward urbanisation completely alters the landscape is essential to the region's climate resilience.

Explore the Land Study
03
Integrated Biodiversity & Art Commons

The Roerich & Devaki Estate, Tataguni

Roerich & Devaki Estate · Kanakapura Road

The state-acquired Tataguni Estate represents a priceless intersection of rich forestry, local heritage, and world-class art. A collaborative master plan to revive its deteriorating structures and depleting flora creates a cultural-ecological sanctuary that protects the southern frontier.

The Campaign
Manifesto

The expanding south and west frontiers of Bengaluru offer a beautiful, active canvas to manifest a different vision. By learning from the land and aligning our new neighbourhoods with the earth's natural water paths and valleys, we can build a balanced city.

The Campaign Perspective

When we nurture existing wetlands, protect natural stream corridors, and integrate vibrant cultural commons, these spaces cease to be isolated patches. They seamlessly connect to form a resilient, city-wide ecological network that supports a healthy microclimate and brings long-term balance to our people, planet, and local economies.

"Humankind has not woven the web of life; we are but one single thread within it. Whatever we do to the web, we do to ourselves. All things are bound together."

For the City

Every zone that systematically conserves wetlands, keeps stream paths open, and integrates cultural common spaces is no longer an isolated patch. It becomes a vital, defensive anchor in a city-wide ecological shield that does not yet exist — but absolutely could.

This is our mission.

To build the next generation of lung spaces in Bengaluru's growth corridor. Not cities plugged into grids. Cities grown into their landscapes.
